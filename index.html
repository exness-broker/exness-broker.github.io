Part 2: What Is Exness Broker & How Does It Work?

Exness Broker acts as an electronic intermediary connecting individual traders to global liquidity providers. Rather than buying physical shares of Apple, physical bars of gold, or actual foreign banknotes, you trade CFDs (Contracts for Difference).

1. How CFD Trading Works on Exness

When you trade on Exness broker, you are entering a financial contract to exchange the difference in an asset's price from the moment you open a position to the moment you close it:
• Long Position (Buy): You open a Buy order when you expect the asset's price to rise. You earn a profit if the price increases and take a loss if it falls.
• Short Position (Sell): You open a Sell order when you expect the asset's price to drop. You earn a profit if the price falls and take a loss if it rises.

2. Exness Revenue Model (Spreads & Commissions)

Exness offers two distinct pricing models depending on the account type you choose:
• Spread-Only Accounts (Standard & Standard Cent): You pay $0 commission per trade. Exness earns its fee from the spread—the minor gap between the Bid (sell) price and the Ask (buy) price, starting from 0.2 to 0.3 pips.
• Raw Spread & Zero Accounts: Designed for scalpers and algorithmic traders, these accounts offer ultra-tight spreads starting from 0.0 pips during peak liquidity hours, paired with a fixed flat commission (typically up to $3.50 per side per standard lot).

3. Instant Automated Withdrawals & Swap-Free Trading

• Extended Swap-Free Status: Normally, holding a leveraged trade overnight incurs a daily interest charge called a swap. Exness automatically grants Swap-Free status to most retail accounts on major forex pairs, gold, crypto, and indices, allowing swing traders to hold positions for days or weeks without overnight rollover fees.

4. Dynamic Leverage & Negative Balance Protection

Exness offers flexible leverage up to 1:2000 (and Unlimited Leverage on certain equity tiers for experienced traders).
• How Leverage Impacts Margin: Leverage lets you control a large market position using a small deposit (margin). For example, at 1:1000 leverage, $100 in margin controls a $100,000 position.
